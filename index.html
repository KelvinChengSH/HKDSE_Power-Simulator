HKDSE Physics Concept Revision & Exam Focus

Key Physics Relationships for HKDSE

  • Slope of T-t Graph: $\text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta T}{\Delta t} = \frac{P \cdot \eta}{m \cdot c}$. Higher power $P$ or smaller mass $m$ leads to a steeper gradient.
  • Heat Loss & Efficiency ($\eta$): In practical HKDSE experiments, the measured $c$ is usually larger than the actual value because some energy $E$ escapes into surroundings without raising water temp.
  • Flat Line at 100°C (Boiling): When water reaches $100^\circ\text{C}$, temperature stops rising ($\Delta T = 0$). Useful energy turns into latent heat of vaporization ($Q = m L_v$) to break intermolecular bonds without increasing kinetic energy.

HKDSE Exam Concept Questions

Q1: Why does the slope of the T-t graph decrease if mass of water is doubled?
Since $\text{Slope} = \frac{P \eta}{m c}$, slope is inversely proportional to mass $m$. Doubling $m$ doubles the heat capacity ($m c$), requiring twice as much energy per degree temp rise, halving the slope.
Q2: What happens to energy transfer when temperature reaches 100°C?
Temperature remains constant at $100^\circ\text{C}$. The electrical energy supplied is absorbed as latent heat of vaporization ($Q = m L_v$) to separate water molecules completely, producing steam.
Q3: How does heat loss (η < 100%) affect experimental determination of c?
If heat loss is ignored ($E_{supplied}$ assumed $= Q_{absorbed}$), $c_{exp} = \frac{P t}{m \Delta T}$. Since actual $Q_{absorbed} < P t$, the calculated $c_{exp}$ will be overestimated (larger than theoretical $4200 \text{ J kg}^{-1} \text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$).
